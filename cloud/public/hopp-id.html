Your Hopp ID

How your account works

Hopp doesn't ask for your email or phone number, and it never keeps your password. Instead, your account is a key that lives on your computer. Here's what that means, and why you get 24 words.

Your account is a key

When you make a Hopp ID, your computer makes a key: a long secret number that only you have. Every time you join a server or talk to a friend, Hopp proves it's you with that key, without ever sending the key itself. Servers and friends only ever see a public part of it, which is safe to share.

The key is kept on your computer, locked with your password. Your password never leaves your computer, and it's never sent to Hopp Cloud or anyone else.

So why the 24 words?

Because nobody else holds your password, nobody can send you a "reset your password" email. That's on purpose: it means nobody can trick a support person into giving away your account, and a leak somewhere can't expose your password.

The 24 words are your key written out in plain words. With them you can:

  • Get back in if you forget your password, and pick a new one
  • Use your Hopp ID on a new or second computer
  • Get your account back if your computer breaks or is replaced

Without them, and without your password, nobody can get the account back. Not you, and not Hopp.

It's not a crypto thing

The words come from the same kind of word list that some crypto wallets use, because it's a good way to write a key in words people can copy by hand without mistakes. That's all. There's no crypto, no wallet, no coins and no money in Hopp.

Keeping it safe

Where to keep your words

  • Best: on paper. Write the 24 words down, in order, and keep the paper somewhere at home.
  • Or as a file, if you'd rather: Hopp can save them as a file you put somewhere private, like a USB stick or a password manager. You don't have to; it's only there if it suits you better.

Never keep them in a chat, an email, a screenshot you share, or a note others can see.

Hopp asks you to type a few of the words back once, so you know you have them right. Until you've saved them, a small reminder stays in Hopp so you don't forget.

What Hopp will never do

  • Hopp, and anyone who works on it, will never ask for your 24 words or your password
  • There's no "log in with a QR code" that someone could trick you into scanning
  • If you paste your words into a chat, Hopp stops and warns you before anything is sent

If anyone asks for your words, for any reason, it's someone trying to take your account.

Day to day

Most of the time you never think about any of this. Tick Keep me logged in and Hopp opens straight into your account. On Windows you can use Windows Hello (your face, fingerprint or PIN) instead of your password when Hopp asks to confirm it's you.

If you lose everything

If you lose both your password and your 24 words, the account can't be recovered. You can make a new Hopp ID right away and add your friends again, but your old account, its friends and its messages stay out of reach. That's the price of nobody else holding the keys, and it's why the words matter.
